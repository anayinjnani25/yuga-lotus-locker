# Yuga Lotus Locker: explainer video script

**Audience:** teachers without technical knowledge · **Voice:** neutral English · **Length:** about 7:00
**Narration:** 940 words, about 6 min 50 s at 145 words per minute (6:25 brisk, 7:20 slow)

## Files to paste into ElevenLabs

| File | Use |
|---|---|
| `video/narration-tagged.txt` | Narration with audio tags (`[curious]`, `[confident]`, `[slowly]`, `[pause]`), written in the Eleven v3 style. **Check that your model supports these tags first**; some models read tags out loud. |
| `video/narration-clean.txt` | The same narration with every tag removed. Safe for any model. |

Both files contain **only** spoken words. All directions are in this document.

**Delivery overall:** warm, calm and curious, like a museum guide speaking to a friend. Medium pace. Smile slightly on the reveals, and slow down for the five-lock sequence and the lotus opening. No hype.

**How to read the visuals column:** numbers like `#21` are the still images in `video/images/` (see `manifest.json`). "Anim" means a short clip I'll render from the 3D model in Phase 6.

## Scene 2: Introduction · 0:27–1:11 · 44 s

**Narration**
> This is the Yuga Lotus Locker: a carved puzzle money box, designed as a school project. [pause] Inside, a copper lotus holds four coins, one for each yuga, the four great ages of Indian tradition: Satya, Treta, Dwapara and Kali. To reach them, a child solves five locks in a set order. Each lock opens the way to the next, so nothing can be skipped. And the box is designed to be built in two ways: an electronic version that guides the child with a small screen and coloured lights, and a mechanical version that runs entirely on springs, with no power at all.

- **Voice:** clear and friendly. Light emphasis on each yuga name, and on "two ways".
- **Visuals:** #01 hero → #37 coin close-up → #02 front.
- **Action:** title card "Yuga Lotus Locker" over the hero. The four yuga names appear one by one in their metal colours. On "two ways", a split screen of #45 (electronic) and #46 (mechanical) with labels.

## Scene 3: Exterior design · 1:11–2:04 · 53 s

**Narration**
> [confident] On the outside, it looks like a temple treasure chest. The front is carved with a temple arch, called a torana. One side carries a lotus mandala, the other a wheel inspired by the Konark sun temple, and the base is cut with a jaali lattice, like the stone screens of old Indian palaces. The lid is a lotus mandala with four small wheels, and brass runs around the plinth, the feet and the corners. [pause] Turn the box around, and the back is clear acrylic, so you can watch the whole mechanism at work. At the front of the base sit a keypad and a small screen, and below them, a narrow slot. Keep that slot in mind. We'll come back to it.

- **Voice:** confident and admiring. A small knowing smile on "Keep that slot in mind."
- **Visuals:** #12 torana → #14 mandala → #13 chakra → #18 jaali → #15 lid → #19 foot → anim: the box turning to the back → #03 rear → #16 keypad and screen → #17 money slot.
- **Action:** a name label fades in under each carving. Slow 3D turnaround to the back. A brass circle highlights the slot and holds.

## Scene 4: The locks

### 4a · Lock one: the code · 2:04–2:44 · 40 s

**Narration**
> [curious] So how does a child get in? [pause] Lock one is a code. In the electronic box, the child types one, seven, zero, nine on the keypad. The screen shows a star for each number, and when the code is right, a tiny motor swings a latch out of the way, and the lid is free. In the mechanical box, four number wheels do the same job. Only when all four show the right numbers can a spring-loaded bar slide free, and a small spring from a ballpoint pen pops the lid up.

- **Voice:** inviting on the question. Read the digits slowly and separately.
- **Visuals:** #16 → anim: keys pressed and the LCD filling with stars → anim: the lid lifting (#21) → #22 dial wheels.
- **Action:** a "LOCK 1 · CODE" chip. The digits 1 7 0 9 appear in sync with the voice. Small labels "Electronic" and "Mechanical" switch between the two sides.

### 4b · Lock two: the walls · 2:44–3:12 · 28 s

**Narration**
> Lock two is the walls. In the mechanical box, springs taken from wooden clothespins fold the four carved walls outward by themselves, and the box opens like a flower. In the electronic box, the child simply folds them down. [pause] Inside each wall is a painted panel: the name of one age, a picture of a sacred bull, and a few facts about that age.

- **Voice:** gentle wonder on "opens like a flower".
- **Visuals:** anim: the walls folding open → #23 the flower → #24 Satya panel → #25 Kali panel.
- **Action:** a "LOCK 2 · WALLS" chip. Slow overhead pull-up as the walls open. Push-in on the bull.

### 4c · Lock three: the picture puzzle · 3:12–3:31 · 19 s

**Narration**
> Lock three is a picture puzzle. Three bands turn around a central tube. Each band carries one slice of four pictures, and the pictures are whole in only one position. When everything lines up, small notches in the bottom band uncover four hidden sockets.

- **Voice:** clear and steady.
- **Visuals:** #26 scrambled → anim: the bands turning → #27 solved → #28 sockets.
- **Action:** a "LOCK 3 · PICTURES" chip. A soft click sound as each band lands. A highlight ring on the sockets.

### 4d · Lock four: leaves and ring · 3:31–3:56 · 25 s

**Narration**
> Lock four uses four bronze peepal leaves. Each leaf has a peg shaped as a circle, a triangle, a square or a star, so it fits only its own socket. Then the leaves become handles: the only way to turn a brass ring until a hole in the ring lines up with a hole in the tube.

- **Voice:** a small rhythm on the four shapes.
- **Visuals:** anim: the leaves flying from the walls into their sockets → #29 leaf → anim: the ring turning → #30 holes aligned.
- **Action:** a "LOCK 4 · LEAVES" chip. The four shape icons pop up in sync with the words. A dotted line joins the two holes as they meet.

## Scene 5: How the lotus opens · 3:56–4:53 · 57 s

**Narration**
> [slowly] And that hole is where lock five begins. [pause] A button slides through both holes. In the mechanical box, it nudges a small lever called a catch. The catch has been holding a rod down against a strong spring, a little like a jack-in-the-box. Once the catch lets go, the spring pushes the rod up by two or three centimetres. In the electronic box, the button presses a tiny switch instead, and a small motor lifts the same rod, slowly and smoothly. [pause] At the top of the rod, a collar and four thin struts work like the ribs of an umbrella. As the rod rises, they push four copper petals open. [pause] And on each petal sits a coin: gold for Satya, silver for Treta, copper for Dwapara, and iron for Kali.

- **Voice:** slower and building, the climax. A soft smile on "jack-in-the-box". Name each coin warmly.
- **Visuals:** #31 → #32 → #33 (the trigger sequence; an animated version is better) → #34 servo (spotlit) → #35 lotus closed → anim: the lotus opening → #36 → #37 coin.
- **Action:** a "LOCK 5 · LOTUS" chip. Arrows show the button pushing, the catch swinging and the rod rising. A small badge reads "Cutaway: the walls are hidden to show the inside". On each coin name, a label appears in that metal's colour.

## Scene 6: Using it, and the piggy bank · 4:53–5:46 · 53 s

**Narration**
> For a child, every step comes with a little help. In the electronic version, the screen says what to do next: solve the puzzle, attach the leaves, push the button. Lights under the lotus glow in each age's metal colour as the leaves go in, and a short tune plays when the lotus opens. [pause] Now, remember that narrow slot below the screen? It's a money slot. Coins and folded notes can be saved at any time, even while the box is locked. They drop into a treasury drawer, and that drawer opens only when the lotus does. The same rising rod pulls a string that releases the drawer's catch, and a spring pushes the drawer out. So the savings become the final reward.

- **Voice:** warm and practical. A playful lift on "remember that narrow slot?"
- **Visuals:** #44 LCD → #38 lights glowing → #17 slot → anim: a note going in through the slot → #41 release string (spotlit) → anim: the drawer sliding out → #39 → #40.
- **Action:** the LCD text shown large on screen. A callout loop links the rising rod to the string and the drawer. A soft chime as the drawer opens.

## Scene 7: What makes it special · 5:46–6:26 · 40 s

**Narration**
> [confident] What makes this box special? First, the story. As the ages pass, the sacred bull stands on four legs, then three, then two, then one. Children see the idea, not just read about it. Second, the order. Every lock depends on the one before, so patience becomes part of the puzzle. Third, the choice. The same design can be built with simple springs and card, or with a small computer and sensors. And for teachers, the electronic box has a test mode and a hidden manual release, so a stuck box is never a problem.

- **Voice:** confident. Count off "First… Second… Third…" clearly. Reassuring on the last line.
- **Visuals:** four bull panels side by side (crops from the inner panels, or the Yugas page art) → #45 / #46 exploded split → #43 wiring only.
- **Action:** three numbered cards build up (Story · Order · Choice). The legs counter animates 4 → 3 → 2 → 1.

## Accuracy notes

- Everything narrated is in the parts data, the build manual or the firmware. Nothing in the narration claims the box was tested or measured.
- Sizes are left out on purpose, because most are still TBD. The one exception is the rod's 2–3 cm rise, which comes from the manual.
- Scene 5 shows cutaway views (hidden walls, a ghosted box). The on-screen badge says so, so viewers know it's an illustration of the inside.
